Calculation Method

Suspension Statics Lab resolves external forces and couples into axial member forces and chassis support reactions. It uses the imported hardpoint geometry for equilibrium and the selected sections for linear-elastic stress, strain, and length change.

Structural model

The vehicle axes point forward in +X, left in +Y, and up in +Z. Hardpoint positions enter equilibrium in meters, forces in newtons, and moments in newton-meters. Member lengths and circular section dimensions are reported in millimeters.

The model assigns hardpoints to the fixed chassis, to moving rigid bodies such as uprights, or to ideal pin nodes. Each two-force member acts along the line between two hardpoints. A standard wishbone is represented by two axial members with a common outer joint. When a pushrod attaches to a wishbone, the model treats that wishbone as a rigid body, hinged at the two chassis pickups and connected to the upright at a ball joint. A rocker with an active damper path rotates about the axis from ROCKER_A to ROCKER_B. For the standard model, an inboard pushrod with no defined damper path is held at its chassis hardpoint.

Equilibrium equations

Each free rigid body has three force equations and three moment equations. About a reference position \(\mathbf c_j\) on body \(j\), the balances are

\[ \sum_k \mathbf F_k = \mathbf 0,\qquad \sum_k (\mathbf r_k-\mathbf c_j)\times\mathbf F_k + \sum_\ell \mathbf C_\ell = \mathbf 0. \]

Here \(\mathbf r_k\) is the force application point and \(\mathbf C_\ell\) is a pure couple. A free pin node has three force equations. A couple may be entered at a rod end attached to a rigid body, using the right-hand sign convention. The entry applies an external moment to the attached body. The member still transmits axial force only.

Member and joint reactions

For a member from A to B, the length, direction, and endpoint forces on the adjoining bodies are

\[ L_0=\lVert\mathbf r_B-\mathbf r_A\rVert,\qquad \hat{\mathbf u}=\frac{\mathbf r_B-\mathbf r_A}{L_0}, \qquad \mathbf F_A=P\hat{\mathbf u},\quad \mathbf F_B=-P\hat{\mathbf u}. \]

Positive \(P\) pulls the two endpoints toward each other and denotes tension. Negative \(P\) denotes compression. A ball joint provides three force reactions at a shared point and zero reaction couple. An ideal hinge adds two independent reaction couple components in the plane perpendicular to its unit axis \(\hat{\mathbf h}\). Its reaction couple satisfies \(\hat{\mathbf h}\cdot\mathbf C_\mathrm{hinge}=0\), leaving rotation about the axis free. Joint reactions enter the incident bodies with opposite signs.

Chassis support resultant

The support resultant balances all external loads, including forces and couples entered at chassis points. The moment reference \(\mathbf o\) is the center of gravity (CG) when available and the coordinate origin otherwise.

\[ \mathbf R=-\sum_k\mathbf F^{\mathrm{ext}}_k,\qquad \mathbf M_{\mathbf o}=-\left[ \sum_k(\mathbf r_k-\mathbf o)\times\mathbf F^{\mathrm{ext}}_k+ \sum_\ell\mathbf C^{\mathrm{ext}}_\ell \right]. \]

The displayed force and moment describe the combined chassis support. A balanced vehicle load case can give a zero resultant while individual members carry substantial forces.

Matrix and solver checks

Each column of the equilibrium matrix \(K\) represents one unit member force or one unit joint reaction. The rod column contributes \(+\hat{\mathbf u}\) to the A body’s force rows and \(-\hat{\mathbf u}\) to the B body’s force rows, with their corresponding moment contributions. The chassis is fixed and contributes no equilibrium rows.

The rigid-body moment reference \(\mathbf c_j\) is the mean of the incident hardpoints used by the solver. It divides that body’s moment rows by a characteristic length \(\ell_j\), the largest distance from \(\mathbf c_j\) to those points with a lower bound of 1 mm. For a point force \(\mathbf f\) and optional couple \(\mathbf C\), the scaled rigid-body contribution is

\[ \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf f \\ [(\mathbf r-\mathbf c_j)\times\mathbf f+\mathbf C]/\ell_j \end{bmatrix}. \]

Hinge couple columns use perpendicular unit basis vectors multiplied by the moving body’s characteristic length, so the scaled columns are comparable with the other reaction columns. The external load vector \(\mathbf b\) uses the same row scaling.

\[ K\mathbf x=-\mathbf b,\qquad \mathbf e=K\mathbf x+\mathbf b. \]
  • The least-squares solve uses a relative singular-value cutoff of \(10^{-10}\). Rank below the number of columns means the member and joint reactions are non-unique.
  • The maximum absolute scaled residual must satisfy \(\lVert\mathbf e\rVert_\infty \le 10^{-7}\max(1,\lVert\mathbf b\rVert_\infty)\). A larger residual means the load is incompatible with the modeled constraints.
  • Full column rank with rank below the equation count permits a unique reaction vector for compatible loads. Other loads can excite the remaining free directions.

Load cases and contact forces

Direct input applies an XYZ force at a hardpoint or a pure couple to a rigid body. Multiple load entries add linearly. The static preset uses 321 kg and gravity. The acceleration preset combines weight, optional downforce \(D\) at the CG, and the equivalent inertia force for acceleration \(\mathbf a=(a_x,a_y,a_z)\).

\[ \mathbf F_{\mathrm{inertial}}=-m\mathbf a,\qquad W=m(g+a_z)+D,\qquad \mathbf F_{\mathrm{CG}}=(-ma_x,\,-ma_y,\,-W). \]

Use \(g=g_0\) when gravity is enabled and \(g=0\) otherwise, with fixed reference \(g_0=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\). The static preset uses \(\mathbf a=\mathbf 0\) and \(D=0\). The single-wheel bump preset applies a standalone +1800 N vertical contact force at the first available wheel.

Estimated four-wheel shares

With contact positions \(\mathbf p_i=(x_i,y_i,z_i)\) relative to the CG, the preset starts with front axle share \(s_f=-\bar x_r/(\bar x_f-\bar x_r)\), where \(\bar x_f\) and \(\bar x_r\) are the front and rear axle mean X coordinates. It divides each axle share equally between its two wheels, then makes the smallest Euclidean correction to satisfy the unit-load force, roll, and pitch balances. For wheel order FL, FR, RL, RR, the nominal balance matrix \(B_0\) and corrected shares \(\mathbf s\) satisfy

\[ B_0=\begin{bmatrix} 1&1&1&1\\ y_{FL}&y_{FR}&y_{RL}&y_{RR}\\ -x_{FL}&-x_{FR}&-x_{RL}&-x_{RR} \end{bmatrix},\qquad B_0\mathbf s=\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{bmatrix}. \]

The acceleration \(a_y\) sets lateral demand \(ma_y\). The independent tire coefficient \(\mu\) is dimensionless, must be nonnegative, and defaults to 1. The preset checks whether the tires can supply the requested lateral force with the available normal load.

\[ W=\sum_i F_{z,i},\qquad |ma_y|\le\mu W. \]

In the CG acceleration preset, for \(W>0\) and feasible demand, the signed ratio \(k_y=ma_y/W\) distributes \(ma_y\) in proportion to the solved normal loads. Longitudinal force keeps its nominal share. This preset checks the lateral component alone.

\[ F_{x,i}=s_i ma_x,\qquad F_{y,i}=k_y F_{z,i},\qquad \sum_i F_{y,i}=ma_y. \]

With \(W=0\), only zero lateral demand is feasible and the lateral ratio is set to zero. The preset applies the smallest Euclidean correction to \(W\mathbf s\) that satisfies vertical force balance and roll and pitch moment balance about the CG. Lateral force contributes \(-z_i F_{y,i}\) to roll, and longitudinal force contributes \(z_i F_{x,i}\) to pitch.

\[ (B_y)_{:,i}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\y_i-k_y z_i\\-x_i\end{bmatrix}, \qquad B_y\mathbf F_z=\begin{bmatrix}W\\0\\-\sum_i z_i F_{x,i}\end{bmatrix}. \]

The preset balances the lateral inertial force through the tires. Any net yaw moment about the CG appears, with reversed sign, in the chassis support moment. A negative normal load in the estimated distribution triggers a possible wheel lift warning. Contact forces supplied directly or in a measured four-wheel CSV are used as entered.

Project JSON stores the coefficient in case_settings.tire_mu. Earlier files without this field use 1.

Grip circle

Choose the Grip circle preset and drag the point anywhere inside the diagram, with lateral acceleration \(a_y\) on the horizontal axis and longitudinal acceleration \(a_x\) on the vertical axis. Apply the preset to use the selected loads. Both acceleration axes have the same limit \(A\) in m/s². The diagram is stretched to an ellipse on screen, while the allowed acceleration region is a disk in data coordinates.

\[ A=\frac{\mu W}{m},\qquad a_x^2+a_y^2\le A^2,\qquad W=m(g+a_z)+D. \]

When \(A=0\), the allowed point is at the origin. For \(W>0\), the preset uses signed ratios \(r_x=ma_x/W\) and \(r_y=ma_y/W\). Both force components are distributed in proportion to the solved normal load and sum to \(ma_x\) and \(ma_y\), respectively.

\[ F_{x,i}=r_x F_{z,i},\qquad F_{y,i}=r_y F_{z,i},\qquad \sqrt{F_{x,i}^2+F_{y,i}^2}\le\mu F_{z,i}. \]

The roll and pitch moments now both depend on the same solved normal loads. The preset corrects the initial distribution \(W\mathbf s\) to satisfy

\[ (B_{\mathrm{grip}})_{:,i}= \begin{bmatrix}1\\y_i-r_yz_i\\r_xz_i-x_i\end{bmatrix},\qquad B_{\mathrm{grip}}\mathbf F_z=\begin{bmatrix}W\\0\\0\end{bmatrix}. \]

The disk checks the combined tire force allowance. The solver also checks normal loads and whether the suspension can balance the resulting loads at the original hardpoints.

Sections and elastic response

A circular section uses outer diameter \(D_o\), inner diameter \(D_i\), and outer radius \(c=D_o/2\), all in millimeters. Require \(D_o>D_i\ge0\). Set \(D_i=0\) for a solid round section. The example section starts at 19 mm outer diameter, 15 mm inner diameter, and a Young’s modulus of 205 GPa. \(I\) and \(S\) are taken about any centroidal diameter axis. The polar properties use the section center.

PropertyEquationUnit
Area\(A=\pi(D_o^2-D_i^2)/4\)mm²
Second moment of area\(I=\pi(D_o^4-D_i^4)/64\)mm⁴
Section modulus\(S=I/c\)mm³
Radius of gyration\(r=\sqrt{I/A}\)mm
Polar second moment\(J=2I\)mm⁴
Polar section modulus\(Z_p=J/c\)mm³
Polar radius of gyration\(r_p=\sqrt{J/A}\)mm

With \(P\) in N, \(A\) in mm², Young’s modulus \(E\) converted from GPa to MPa, and \(L_0\) in mm, the reported axial values are

\[ \sigma=\frac{P}{A},\qquad \varepsilon=\frac{\sigma}{E},\qquad \Delta L=\varepsilon L_0=\frac{P L_0}{AE}. \]

Stress is in MPa, strain is displayed in microstrain \(10^6\varepsilon\), and length change is in mm. Compression gives negative stress, strain, and length change. Section properties affect this post-processing. The equilibrium solve uses only geometry, topology, and external loads.

The displayed geometry stays at its original hardpoints. Evaluating bending, torsion, buckling, large deflection, material yielding, or fatigue requires additional structural analysis.

Rod interference

During statics and history solves, the program checks pairs of actual axial members at the imported hardpoint pose. Each rod is approximated by a capsule with radius \(r_i=D_{o,i}/2\) about its finite three-dimensional centerline. If \(\mathbf p_i(s)=\mathbf a_i+s(\mathbf b_i-\mathbf a_i)\) parameterizes the centerline under test, the minimum centerline distance and estimated overlap are

\[ d_{\min}=\min_{0\le s,t\le1}\lVert\mathbf p_i(s)-\mathbf p_j(t)\rVert,\qquad \delta=r_i+r_j-d_{\min}. \]

A positive overlap produces a warning with both member names and the estimated overlap in millimeters. Tangent capsules are treated as touching. To allow for an intended shared joint, each centerline is shortened at that end by \(2(r_i+r_j)\) before checking the rest of the pair. Coincident members are still checked.

The warning leaves the equilibrium results intact. The estimate covers rod bodies at the reference pose. Checking rigid parts, actual joint envelopes, or clearance throughout suspension travel requires their geometry and motion.

Force history

An acceleration ramp uses 101 equally spaced samples from zero acceleration to the selected acceleration. At time \(t\) in a ramp of duration \(T\), \(\mathbf a(t)=(t/T)\mathbf a_\mathrm{target}\). Each sample uses the selected mode, CG acceleration or Grip circle, with the same hardpoints, tire coefficient, gravity setting, and enabled CG downforce. Changing the duration changes the time scale and acceleration slope.

A lap CSV uses strictly increasing time_s and at least two rows. A complete set of four-wheel XYZ force channels is used directly. An acceleration-only CSV uses the selected mode and the current mass, gravity, tire coefficient, and enabled downforce. An omitted vertical acceleration is treated as zero for that estimate. An incompatible frame is reported with its timestamp.

The signed peak is the sample with the largest absolute force and retains its sign. The two means are time-weighted and use piecewise-linear interpolation, including exact integration of the two triangles when the force crosses zero inside an interval.

\[ \bar P=\frac{1}{t_n-t_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_n}P(t)\,\mathrm dt,\qquad \overline{|P|}=\frac{1}{t_n-t_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_n}|P(t)|\,\mathrm dt. \]

LP03 shared damper

The Kinematics CSV jacobian_project field can define deformable_members with a name, two endpoint positions in meters, and two mounts. In LP03_rockerset.csv, DAMPER_1 extends from the right front rocker (A) to the left front rocker (B). Its forces act with opposite signs on the two rockers, each with its own moment arm about its pivot axis.

\[ \tau_R + P\left[(\mathbf r_A-\mathbf q_R)\times\hat{\mathbf u}\right]\cdot\hat{\mathbf h}_R=0, \] \[ \tau_L - P\left[(\mathbf r_B-\mathbf q_L)\times\hat{\mathbf u}\right]\cdot\hat{\mathbf h}_L=0. \]

Here \(\mathbf q_R\) and \(\mathbf q_L\) are points on the right and left pivot axes, and \(\tau_R\) and \(\tau_L\) are the other torques about those axes. Both equations must agree on the same \(P\). With the supplied LP03 geometry, the standard vertical static-wheel preset fails the equilibrium check. A compatible load or a physical load path for the remaining motion is needed to obtain a static solution. In a controlled check, equal and opposite 500 N forces pulling the endpoints apart along the rod axis give \(P=+500\ \mathrm N\).

The imported damper is evaluated as an axial member at the specified pose. Its velocity-dependent damping force, gas preload, and spring characteristics are outside this calculation.
